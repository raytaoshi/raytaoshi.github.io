Hash Functions and Digital Signatures

Cryptography Knowledge · Last updated August 2026

Hash functions create fixed-length digests; digital signatures use private keys to authenticate messages and protect their integrity.

Cryptographic hashes

Important properties include preimage resistance, second-preimage resistance, and collision resistance. Hashes are not encryption and do not conceal their input.

Message authentication

A keyed MAC verifies integrity and authenticity between parties sharing a secret. Raw unkeyed hashes generally cannot authenticate a message.

Digital signatures

A signer uses a private key and verifiers use the corresponding public key. Secure use depends on the signature scheme, domain separation, message encoding, key validation, and trustworthy binding between key and identity.
