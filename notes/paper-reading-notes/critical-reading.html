Critical Reading and Evidence Evaluation

Paper Reading Notes · Last updated August 2026

A claim is only as strong as the design, measurement, and comparison that support it.

Interrogate the claim

Rewrite the main claim in precise language. Note its population, task, metric, operating conditions, and implied causal direction. Broad wording often hides narrow evidence.

Inspect the evidence

  • Are datasets, baselines, and metrics appropriate for the stated question?
  • Are comparisons controlled and uncertainty reported?
  • Do ablations isolate the component credited for the improvement?
  • Could leakage, selection effects, or unreported tuning explain the result?

Calibrate the conclusion

Distinguish observation, interpretation, and speculation. Then record the strongest supported conclusion and the most important unresolved alternative. Capture the result using the research note template.
